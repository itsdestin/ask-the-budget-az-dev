**Analysis: How AHCCCS enrollment undershot the budget, and the FY 2027 ripple** The short version: AHCCCS enrollment ran far below what the budget assumed, producing real General Fund savings on the caseload side — but those savings were swamped by larger-than-budgeted capitation rate increases, leaving a formula funding shortfall that required a FY 2026 supplemental. The FY 2027 budget then had to remove that one-time supplemental, build continued enrollment declines into its formulas, and backfill fund shifts that had shored up the General Fund in FY 2025–26. **1. What happened: enrollment came in well under budget** The budget had assumed enrollment would resume growth once AHCCCS completed its pandemic-era disenrollment workload in June 2024. Instead, caseloads kept falling: | | Budgeted | What actually happened | General Fund effect | |---|---|---|---| | FY 2025 | 1.1% enrollment growth | effectively a (1.1)% decline; June 2025 enrollment (8.2)% below June 2023 | $(55,329,900) in state match savings; $(281,593,800) Total Funds | | FY 2026 | growth of ~15,000, to 1.86 million by June 2026 | decline of (190,000), to 1.65 million by June 2026 | $(105,621,600) in savings vs. the enacted budget | The mechanism: even where the growth-rate assumption held, the declines that occurred before June 2024 kept annualizing, so each year's caseload sat lower than the year before. **2. The offset: capitation increases swamped the savings** The savings did not reach the bottom line. On October 1, 2025, AHCCCS implemented an average capitation rate increase of 5.5% (the enacted budget assumed 3.0%), then on December 11, 2025 raised those rates retroactively by an additional 8.6% — a combined 14.1% adjustment — generating $169,757,700 in General Fund costs versus the enacted budget . AHCCCS attributed the increases to the caseload declines leaving higher-cost members on the program, significant increases in utilization of Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) services, and general medical inflation . Net of the enrollment savings, the FY 2026 budget ended up needing a supplemental of $104,728,400 from the General Fund and $471,741,800 from expenditure authority to cover a projected formula funding shortfall . Within that supplemental, lower-than-anticipated enrollment saved $(105,621,600), the capitation adjustment cost $169,757,700, and $40,592,300 came from other factors such as a provider prepayment review backlog, a tobacco tax revenue decline, and more members with retroactive coverage . **3. Ripple into FY 2027** 1. **The one-time supplemental came out.** The FY 2027 budget removes the FY 2026 one-time supplemental formula funding — $(247,415,800) at the line-item level , shown as $(109.2) million of supplemental and other one-time funding removal in the General Fund summary . 2. **The formulas now assume continued enrollment decline.** FY 2027 formula adjustments embed a (10.3)% FY 2026 decline and a further (2.5)% in FY 2027, with the decline attributed to base demographic changes plus program modifications required by federal H.R. 1 legislation and the state FY 2027 Health Budget Reconciliation Bill . Enrollment is projected at 1.61 million by June 2027, down (232,000), or (12.6)%, from June 2025 . 3. **Backfills replaced the fund shifts.** FY 2027 includes $100.0 million to backfill the one-time use of the Hospital Assessment Fund in FY 2025 and FY 2026, and $50.0 million to backfill the one-time use of the Prescription Drug Rebate Fund . So despite the enrollment declines, FY 2027 AHCCCS General Fund spending still increases by $202.2 million, or 7.4%, because capitation growth and the backfills outweigh the caseload savings . **One timing note:** the FY 2027 Baseline (prepared while FY 2026 was still underway) told an earlier version of this story — it projected the June 2026 caseload at 1.68 million, sized the supplemental at $64,528,400 , and assumed essentially flat enrollment in FY 2027 . The FY 2027 Appropriations Report reflects the finalized numbers after FY 2026 closed: the actual 1.65 million June 2026 caseload, the larger $104.7 million supplemental, and the further FY 2027 decline driven by H.R. 1 and the Health Care BRB .
