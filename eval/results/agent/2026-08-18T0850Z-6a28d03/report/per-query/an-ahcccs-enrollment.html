The enrollment decline then carried into **FY 2027 as a base adjustment** to AHCCCS (Arizona Health Care Cost Containment System) formula funding. This is the JLBC FY 2027 Baseline telling of it. **What happened in FY 2026** The enacted budget assumed AHCCCS enrollment would grow by about **15,000** over FY 2026 and reach **1.86 million** by June 2026 . Instead, enrollment **fell by 107,000 in just the first six months** of FY 2026, and JLBC projected the June 2026 caseload at **1.68 million — 164,000 below June 2025** . Those declines were expected to generate **$104,672,500 in General Fund savings** versus the enacted budget . That savings was more than offset on the cost side. On October 1, 2025, AHCCCS implemented an average capitation rate increase of **5.5%** (the enacted budget assumed 3.0%) and then announced a further retroactive increase; JLBC estimated the combined increase at **14.4%**, generating **$157,908,300 in General Fund costs** above the enacted budget . AHCCCS attributed the rate pressure to a variety of factors: the caseload declines left members with higher-than-average health care costs on the program, utilization of Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) services rose significantly, and there was general medical inflation .
