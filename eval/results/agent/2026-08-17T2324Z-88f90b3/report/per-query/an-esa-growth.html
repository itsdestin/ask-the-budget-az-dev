**Analysis:** School vouchers — Arizona's Empowerment Scholarship Account (ESA) program — have reshaped the K-12 funding formula from a minor line item into one of the fastest-growing costs in the state budget, and the state's enrollment projections have consistently underestimated actual participation, especially after universal eligibility opened. ## The cost trajectory **Before universal expansion (through FY 2022):** The ESA program was a comparatively small component of K-12 formula spending. ESAs were funded primarily with Basic State Aid monies that a school district or charter school otherwise would have received for a student if they had remained in public school . In FY 2021, ADE (Arizona Department of Education) reported roughly 9,200 enrollees at a total cost of about $145,000,000, or an average of $15,761 per recipient . The FY 2022 enacted budget projected total ESA costs of $155,538,000 for 10,572 enrollees . **Universal eligibility changes everything (FY 2023 onward):** Laws 2022, Chapter 388 expanded ESA eligibility to any Arizona student eligible to enroll in a public school . Critically, the enacted FY 2023 budget did not include any adjustments to Basic State Aid funding to account for universal ESAs, because the program was enacted in separate legislation outside the budget . The FY 2023 Appropriations Report had projected ESA enrollment of 13,200 — but the footnote explicitly noted this "does not reflect enrollment impacts of Laws 2022, Chapter 388" . By the time the FY 2024 Baseline was written, actual FY 2023 ESA enrollment had reached 56,912, with total program expenses of $474,231,400 — an increase of $274,063,300 above the $200,168,100 originally budgeted . This drove a $200,000,000 supplemental appropriation for FY 2023 for higher-than-budgeted K-12 formula expenses . **The cost escalation since:** | Fiscal Year | Projected ESA Enrollment | Projected Total Awards | Source | |---|---|---|---| | FY 2023 (original budget) | 13,200 | ~$200,168,100 | FY 2024 Baseline | | FY 2023 (actual) | 56,912 | $474,231,400 | FY 2024 Baseline | | FY 2024 (enacted) | 68,380 | — | FY 2024 Approps | | FY 2025 (enacted) | 82,452 | $824,530,600 | FY 2025 Approps | | FY 2026 (enacted) | 97,905 | $1,001,720,000 | FY 2026 Approps | | FY 2027 (enacted) | 113,602 | $1,215,678,900 | FY 2027 Approps | From roughly $145 million in FY 2021 to over $1.2 billion projected in FY 2027, ESA costs have grown by more than 8× in six years. The FY 2027 budget alone includes a $154,170,300 General Fund increase for ESA enrollment growth . ## Were the enrollment guesses right? **No — and the documents say so repeatedly.** JLBC (Joint Legislative Budget Committee) staff consistently flagged that "all ESA projections remain highly speculative" , and the FY 2024 Appropriations Report noted that "we consider both ADE's estimates and the enacted budget estimates to be highly speculative because the future participation rate of newly-eligible students is difficult to determine in advance" . The clearest miss: the FY 2023 enacted budget assumed 13,200 ESA enrollees , but actual enrollment reached 56,912 — more than 4× the budgeted figure . The FY 2024 enacted budget assumed 68,380 total ESA enrollees , while ADE was already reporting 61,095 approved enrollees as of June 2023 and estimating 100,000 by the end of FY 2024 . **The "switcher" assumption was also too low.** A key fiscal question is what share of new ESA recipients were previously in public school (and therefore generate offsetting Basic State Aid savings) versus already in private school or homeschool (purely new cost to the state). The FY 2025 enacted budget assumed 48% of new ESA enrollees were public-school switchers, while ADE reported that approximately 61% of new entrants in FY 2024 had been enrolled in a public school the year before . The FY 2025 Baseline assumed an even lower 40% switcher rate . The more switchers there are, the more the ESA cost is offset by public-school savings — but the majority of growth is still coming from students who were never in the public system, meaning those costs are net new to the state. **Public school enrollment is dropping faster than demographics predict.** The FY 2025 Baseline found that the estimated public school enrollment decline of (1.0)% in FY 2024 was (1.2)% below the 0.2% growth that demographic projections from OEO (Office of Economic Opportunity) would have anticipated, which "may indicate that ESA switchers are impacting public school enrollment" . Because ESA growth includes pupils not previously funded in public schools, JLBC anticipated that combined ESA-plus-public-school enrollment growth would exceed OEO's school-age population estimates — meaning the state is now funding more total students than demographic trends alone would predict . ## The bottom line Universal ESA eligibility turned a $200 million program into a $1.2 billion program in four years, and the state's budget projections lagged behind reality at every step. The documents are candid that these projections are "highly speculative" , and the combination of under-projected enrollment and a higher-than-assumed share of net-new students (not switchers) means ESA costs have consistently exceeded what was budgeted, requiring mid-year supplemental appropriations and upward revisions in every subsequent budget cycle.
