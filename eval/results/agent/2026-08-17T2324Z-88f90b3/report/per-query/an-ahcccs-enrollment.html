**Analysis: AHCCCS Medicaid Enrollment vs. Budget — The "Unwinding" and Its Multi-Year Ripple** The story begins with the COVID-19 public health emergency (PHE). Under the federal Families First Coronavirus Response Act, AHCCCS was prohibited from disenrolling members who exceeded income eligibility limits while the PHE was in effect. That prohibition continued by federal executive action until April 1, 2023, at which point AHCCCS resumed disenrolling members pursuant to the 2023 Consolidated Appropriations Act . This "Medicaid unwinding" is the single event that drove enrollment below budgeted levels and rippled through the next two fiscal years. --- ### FY 2024 — Enrollment comes in lower than budgeted The enacted FY 2024 budget assumed the expiration of continuous enrollment would bring AHCCCS enrollment gradually back to pre-pandemic levels by June 2024, adjusted for state population growth . The FY 2024 Baseline had assumed a (19.5)% enrollment decline ; the FY 2024 Appropriations Report later revised that to (13.6)% . What actually happened was more nuanced than a uniform decline: - **Traditional Medicaid enrollment declined faster than anticipated** — fewer members than the budget expected, which produced General Fund savings . - **Proposition 204 Childless Adults and KidsCare declined slower than anticipated** — more members than budgeted, which partially offset the Traditional savings . - **Fee-for-service per-member spending came in lower than originally anticipated**, adding further savings . The net result: overall AHCCCS enrollment in June 2024 was actually *higher* than originally anticipated (because some populations declined more slowly), but overall General Fund formula expenses came in $(39,944,900) below the enacted budget assumption . The faster Traditional Medicaid declines outweighed the slower declines in other populations. This triggered an **FY 2024 base adjustment** — a downward rebase of the spending base carried into FY 2025 — of $(126,626,500) in state match and $(546,838,600) in Total Funds, primarily associated with lower-than-budgeted caseloads and the decline in fee-for-service expenditures . --- ### FY 2025 — The rebase flows through; growth assumption becomes a decline The FY 2025 budget originally assumed that once AHCCCS completed its pandemic-related disenrollment workload in June 2024, enrollment would grow at 1.1% (based on Arizona population growth projections) . But because June 2024 enrollment came in higher than the budget had anticipated, applying that 1.1% growth rate to the actual (higher) base meant the June 2025 caseload level was still lower than June 2024 — effectively a (1.1)% enrollment *decline* in FY 2025 . Despite the headline growth assumption, the annualization of caseload declines that occurred *prior* to June 2024 continued to produce savings. June 2025 caseload was projected to be (1.1)% below June 2024 and (8.2)% below the June 2023 peak . Overall, caseload declines decreased state match expenses by $(55,329,900) and Total Funds expenses by $(281,593,800) in FY 2025 . --- ### FY 2026 — Annualization continues to generate savings The FY 2026 Appropriations Report assumed 1.2% enrollment growth . But the annualization of caseload declines that occurred *throughout FY 2025* continued to pull spending below prior-year levels, resulting in further General Fund caseload savings . Overall, state match expenses decreased by $(186,401,300) and Total Funds expenses by $(127,391,800) in FY 2026 . --- ### Summary | Fiscal Year | Headline Enrollment Assumption | What Actually Happened | State Match Savings | Total Funds Savings | |---|---|---|---|---| | FY 2024 | (19.5)% decline (Baseline) / (13.6)% (Approps) | Traditional Medicaid declined faster than budgeted; Prop 204 & KidsCare slower; FFS spending lower | $(353,509,900) | $(2,390,722,400) | | FY 2025 | 1.1% growth → effectively (1.1)% decline | Annualization of pre-June 2024 declines; June 2025 still 8.2% below June 2023 | $(55,329,900) | $(281,593,800) | | FY 2026 | 1.2% growth | Annualization of FY 2025 declines continued | $(186,401,300) | $(127,391,800) | The mechanism is "annualization": even when a budget year *assumes* positive growth, if the starting enrollment base has fallen sharply (as it did during the unwinding), spending for that year is still lower than the prior year's because the lower caseload is being annualized across all twelve months. That is why FY 2025 and FY 2026 both *assume growth* yet still generate savings — the declines happened earlier, and their fiscal echo persists. --- Want me to draft this as a memo, or drill into a specific AHCCCS population (Traditional, Prop 204, KidsCare, ALTCS)?
