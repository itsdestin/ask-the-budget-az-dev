**Comparison:** Funding for both building and maintaining Arizona state highways has **not** been holding steady over the last few years — construction spending jumped sharply in FY 2024, and the maintenance appropriation has risen each year. Looking first at **total cash highway construction expenditures** (across all sources — State Highway Fund, federal aid, Maricopa Regional Area Road Fund, bond proceeds, and the Local Agency Deposits Fund), the FY 2025 Baseline shows the swing: $1,340.7 million in FY 2023, climbing to $1,983.9 million in FY 2024, then $1,918.9 million in FY 2025 . The comparable FY 2023 figures in the FY 2023 Approps Report are consistent — cash construction spending of $1,496.1 million in FY 2022 and $1,681.3 million in FY 2023, plus roughly $312 million a year in debt service . So the trajectory is a substantial ramp-up, not a steady plateau. **Highway maintenance** appropriations (the amount exempted from lapsing and funded from the State Highway Fund) have also grown year over year: | Fiscal year | Highway maintenance appropriation | |---|---| | FY 2025 | $160,760,600 | | FY 2026 | $182,913,000 | | FY 2027 | $201,247,900 | The FY 2027 Executive Budget adds more one-time maintenance money ($10.2 million for new highway lane-miles) and expand-the-system explainers, noting the State system is anticipated to grow by 413 new urban lane-miles and 74 new rural lane-miles in FY 2026 . One structural caveat worth flagging: only a small portion of the state's total highway construction funding is actually appropriated — most comes from SHF beginning balances, bond revenues, federal funds, MRARF, and HURF that are not part of the annual appropriation . So the figures above measure total construction activity, while the separately-appropriated SHF amounts are a much smaller slice of that picture.
